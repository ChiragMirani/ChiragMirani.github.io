Why I built it

I have spent about 20 years in quantitative finance, and I have a Master of Science in Computational Finance from Carnegie Mellon University. None of that makes the Series 65 easy. The exam is mostly law, ethics and definitions, and most questions come down to two answers that both sound right.

When I started studying, the practice questions in my prep course were hard, which is fine, but the explanations mostly told me the right letter. They rarely told me why the answer I picked was wrong, and that is the part I kept repeating. So I started writing my own questions, and for each one I wrote down the trap behind every wrong answer and the rule that settles it, with a link to the source so I could check it.

That notebook grew into this app. I studied with it for about two months and passed the Series 65 the first time, in September 2026. Everything in the app is what I used: the same questions, the same explanations and the same study method.

A note on what passing means: the Series 65 is a qualifying exam. Passing it is one step toward registering as an investment adviser representative with a state; it is not a registration or a license on its own.

How many questions are on the Series 65 exam?

140 questions: 130 scored questions plus 10 unscored pretest questions mixed in, so you can't tell which ones don't count. You have 180 minutes, about 77 seconds a question.

What score do you need to pass the Series 65?

You need at least 92 of the 130 scored questions right, which is about 71%. Results are shown on screen right after you finish.

What topics does the Series 65 cover?

Four sections, from the North American Securities Administrators Association (NASAA) outline that took effect on June 12, 2023: Economic Factors and Business Information (15%, 20 questions), Investment Vehicle Characteristics (25%, 32 questions), Client Investment Recommendations and Strategies (30%, 39 questions), and Laws, Regulations and Guidelines, Including Prohibition on Unethical Business Practices (30%, 39 questions).

Who writes and runs the Series 65 exam?

NASAA develops the exam and the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) administers it. The exam fee is $187, paid to FINRA. It is closed book.
