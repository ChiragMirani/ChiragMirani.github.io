A Simple Weekly Signal
for the U.S. 10-Year Treasury Yield

A checklist for timing duration adjustments after forming a macro view
Chirag Mirani
Independent Researcher
November 7, 2026 | Working paper, version 5

Abstract

A macro view tells a bond investor why to add or reduce duration. It still leaves a timing decision. I present a simple weekly signal for the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield to help review that decision. Fourteen market, economic and calendar readings form an equal-vote checklist. A logistic regression, refitted every 13 weeks, provides a second view; the combined signal takes a direction when the two do not conflict. Across November 2016-October 2026, the selected model gets 52.9% of 342 active calls right and scores +32.6 yield basis points per year, or +27.2 after an illustrative charge. The refit frequency and 30-year TIPS auction addition were chosen after reviewing the historical results; the paper reports the searches and benchmarks alongside the selected model. A practical example shows how an investor with a bullish macro view can use the readings to add duration, wait or build gradually. The public tool supplies the dated checklist and an accumulating record of weekly outcomes. Scores measure yield direction, not portfolio returns; allocation size remains a portfolio decision.

1. A macro view still leaves a timing decision

I may expect slower growth, lower inflation and an easier Fed to bring yields down over the next few months. That gives me a reason to own more duration. It does not tell me whether to add all of it today, wait for a better entry or build the position in stages.

This checklist is an additional tool for that decision. It brings market prices, economic readings and a known auction date into one weekly view of the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield. I want to see the balance of evidence, which readings disagree with my macro view and whether the statistical model sees the same thing.

The output is a duration view one week ahead: Bullish Duration, Bearish Duration or Neutral. Bullish favors lower yields; bearish favors higher yields. The investor starts with a macro position and a risk budget. Neutral adds no weekly tilt to that plan; it does not mean sell the portfolio or return to benchmark.

Why a simple model? In practice I also use more complex models, including nonlinear methods and ensembles. Here I want the reader to see every input, understand each vote and reproduce the calculation. A checklist and logistic regression make that possible.

2. Fourteen readings, two views, one weekly signal

Model 1 is the checklist. It covers equities, oil, the dollar, volatility, unemployment, inflation, the yield curve, foreign yields, December seasonality and an upcoming 30-year TIPS auction. Each reading contributes +1 for lower yields, -1 for higher yields or zero. The total is the checklist score. Appendix A gives every definition.

Model 2 is a logistic regression. It uses the same information, allowing the size of a move and the weight on each input to differ. It refits every 13 weeks using all available past history. This is a quarterly refit, not a 13-week training window. A model reading above 50% favors lower yields; below 50% favors higher yields.

When the directions agree, I keep the shared view. When they disagree, the combined signal is zero. If the checklist is exactly zero, the regression decides. The calculation is position = sign(sign(checklist score) + sign(probability - 0.5)). Positions are always +1, -1 or zero. The score and probability describe the reading; neither automatically scales exposure.

For the auction input, a 30-year TIPS auction must already have been announced before the reference Friday and occur in the following seven days. It contributes one add-duration vote and a binary regression input. Auction yields, bid-to-cover ratios and other results are never used. This is a calendar relationship selected from the data, rather than an assumption that more supply must lower yields.

3. How I would use it with a bullish macro view

Suppose my portfolio and benchmark both have six years of duration. My bullish macro view supports a target of seven years, within the risk budget. I set that target before looking at the checklist. The weekly reading then helps me review the entry.

5. Finding and testing the inputs

I start with an economic reason to examine a variable, specify its reading and test it on its own. Rising oil, for example, can point to inflation pressure; a stronger dollar can tighten financial conditions. These hypotheses concern the next week, not just the contemporaneous market reaction. Appendix E explains what each input measures and where its interpretation can break down.

I then ask whether a candidate helps the combined model. The initial forward search picks two additions using the early period: Next Friday falls in the first seven days of a month (jobs calendar proxy) (yes = sell); 10-year yield below its 300-day average (yes = sell). It produces a 15-rule model with a later score of 31.9 bp/year. Its paired gain over the annual reference has t = 0.78. The full candidate list and both selection steps remain in the results package.

A broader search tests 2,430 classifier settings and then 1,440 ridge-regression settings. These cover training windows, refit frequency, input sets, thresholds and scaled positions. The in-sample winners score 7.0 and 2.6 bp/year in the later period. They do not improve the reference average. Appendix D reports the grids and winners, including the unsuccessful approaches.

I also test upcoming nominal 20-year, nominal 30-year and 30-year TIPS auctions, plus an any-long-auction flag, in both directions. With annual refits, the early winner is an any-auction reduce-duration vote. With quarterly refits, the early winner is a nominal 30-year add-duration vote, whose later score is 26.3 bp/year. I choose the quarterly 30-year TIPS add-vote version after comparing its later results. Appendices F and G retain every auction variant, so the reader can see the choice in context.

What was decided when. I developed this implementation in November 2026 after inspecting history through October. On November 7 I corrected timing, archive and curve-tie issues and reran the calculations. The first feature search and the two broader searches rank candidates on the early period; the broader searches require every ranking outcome to end before November 1, 2016. I then compare refit frequencies, choose quarterly refitting using the later results, and repeat the auction search on that baseline. Version 4 adopts the 30-year TIPS add-vote variant after reviewing those later results. The 2027 watchlist separately selects directions using 2026 outcomes. These are disclosed research choices: the later period is retrospective evaluation, not an untouched test. Version 5 leaves those rules unchanged and adds checked original-publisher market-data fallbacks. The first prospective call is generated on November 7 from November 6 reference data, for the week ending November 13, 2026.

6. Data timing and strength of evidence

I rebuild unemployment, core PCE inflation and foreign yields from dated ALFRED snapshots. The unemployment comparison uses distinct earlier months; inflation is calculated within one vintage. Foreign-yield rules are neutral before their archives begin. The dollar basket uses six raw exchange rates. Currency and oil observations are delayed seven days. For live updates, missing recent Treasury yields and VIX closes can come directly from Treasury and Cboe after twenty overlapping observations match FRED. Existing history is preserved and actual retrieval times are recorded. Auction flags use announcement dates, with a strict before-Friday cutoff.

These steps address publication timing, but current market histories can still contain corrections or backfills, and not every historical intraday publication time has been recovered. The auction audit verifies a sample of original notices, not the entire calendar. Appendix C gives the calculations and data checks; Appendices F-G describe the event tests.

For the selected model, Newey-West t-statistics are 1.67 with four lags and 1.74 with eight. The simple 95% hit-rate interval is 47.6%-58.2%. These statistics do not adjust for the searches and later-informed choices. The regression's Brier loss is 0.2505, compared with 0.2500 for a constant 50% forecast. I use the probability as a model reading, not a calibrated confidence level or a sizing formula.

The annual-reference placebo gives 43 exceedances in 500 circular shifts, or 0.0878 using (k+1)/(N+1). Repeating the first two-addition search gives 11 in 200, or 0.0597. Those diagnostics do not repeat the later refit and auction choices and are not significance tests for version 4. The input-substitution study also changes economic definitions, so it cannot isolate the effect of revised data alone.

The illustrative cost charge reduces the selected model's score to 27.2 bp/year, counting opening, closing and intermediate position changes. It is a sensitivity assumption, not a measured trading cost. A portfolio evaluation would record actual implementation prices and compare the macro strategy with and without the tactical adjustments. The public record will distinguish those decisions from the model's yield score.

8. The weekly tool and how to follow it

The public signal tool leads with the latest complete dated reading, the balance of add and reduce votes, the regression view and the resulting duration interpretation. The factor table shows the individual votes. The preprint page provides this paper, the code and downloadable historical results.

The page can be checked or refreshed on any day. Its tested horizon remains Friday to Friday. A Monday visit retrieves the existing weekly reading; it does not silently create a Monday-to-Monday forecast. New issues require fresh complete inputs inside the Friday-evening-to-Saturday issue window. Missing inputs hold back a new issue while the last complete reading stays visible with its original dates.

Each published forecast records its actual issue time and model version. Reruns preserve it. Outcomes are added separately as they become available, and the tracker distinguishes historical reconstruction from forecasts actually issued before the outcome. Weekly results are signed yield changes, with flat and unavailable weeks identified separately. The first call uses November 6 reference data and is generated on November 7, 2026, before the following trading week. Its benchmark starts at the Friday reference yield, not at an executable price at issue time.

I record the macro view, target duration, weekly evidence and action taken to see whether the checklist changed the timing. For questions, Get in touch on LinkedIn.

Appendix C. Data, calculations and reproduction

Timing. ALFRED dates identify source, provider or archive availability; they are not a guarantee of each original intraday release. Core PCE growth uses the latest month and the same month a year earlier in one snapshot, including revisions already present there. The unemployment average excludes the current month and uses 60 distinct earlier available months. Before there is enough history, the rule is neutral. Foreign-yield rules are neutral before June 2013. The early model therefore has fewer than thirteen informative inputs.

Archive checks. I reject nonfinite values, duplicate or nonconsecutive months, observations ahead of their vintage, very old latest observations and backwards jumps in the latest month. One German snapshot dated August 1, 2013 ended in December 1969 and is excluded. No revised-data fallback fills the early foreign-yield gaps. Current market histories can still contain revisions or backfills. Treasury reference-yield quotations are around 3:30 p.m., while some other inputs close later.

Calendar rules. The supplied Fed dates are scheduled decisions, but historical announcement-time schedule revisions have not been fully audited. The jobs rule means that next Friday falls in the first seven days of a month. It is a calendar proxy and never reads future realized jobs-release dates. Neither event rule is in the thirteen-rule specification.

Training and scoring. Continuous inputs use an expanding mean and standard deviation, including the current observable value, with at least 52 observations; standardized values are clipped at four and missing values become zero. December and the selected auction flag stay 0/1. Four-week curve changes are rounded to ten decimal places in percentage points before taking a sign, so exact quoted ties stay neutral. At each refit, the regression uses only training weeks whose outcomes have ended by that Friday. Unchanged targets are classified as not down. HAC statistics use an intercept-only regression with Bartlett weights and four lags; eight-lag results are also shown. Volatility and SR use all calendar weeks and annualize with 52. Wilson hit-rate intervals assume independent observations and do not adjust for selection.

Sample boundaries. The original tables define the early period by signal date: the last early signal is October 28, 2016 and its outcome is November 4, before that Friday's next forecast. This convention also applies to the initial two-addition search. The two broader searches impose a stricter information cutoff: all ranking outcomes end before November 1, leaving 564 early signal weeks. The later evaluation has 521 completed weekly outcomes. Circular-shift diagnostics use offsets of at least 52 weeks and compare unrounded statistics; their reported fraction is (k+1)/(N+1).

Reproduction. The package contains the research model, downloader, input-sensitivity check, weekly script, frozen specification, Fed-date file, dependencies and manuscript builder. The improvement_study folder contains both protocols, both search scripts and every trial. The watchlist_2027 folder contains its dated protocol, all 54 direction trials, every rule's ranking and selected weekly scores. The economic_review folder contains interpretation notes for all 27 rules and the post-discussion reversal and curve checks. The auction_study folder contains the additional protocol, official calendar records, announcement audit, timing tests and all eight variants. The separate refit comparison is in improvement_study/refit_comparison.json. README.txt gives the commands. Downloads create a new snapshot and do not overwrite the archived local study inputs. A research run cannot overwrite the production specification. Raw market and macro files are excluded; their hashes identify the archived study inputs. A later download can change the historical results and need not reproduce the paper exactly.

Appendix D. Broader searches and the 2027 watchlist

The five input sets are: the main thirteen; those thirteen without the three foreign yields; seven inputs covering US and EM equities, oil, dollar, VIX, curve and December; thirteen plus breakeven inflation; and thirteen plus the jobs proxy and 300-day yield trend. The last two are named candidates carried into this search, not new discoveries independent of earlier results.

The classification grid uses C = 0.01, 0.1 or 1; all history, 520 weeks or 260 weeks of training; 52- or 13-week refits; minimum absolute checklist scores of 0, 1 or 3; probability distances from 0.5 of 0, 0.025 or 0.05; and three sizing rules. Probability sizing is min(abs(p-0.5)/0.10, 1); checklist sizing is abs(score)/number of rules. The existing combination supplies the direction. These choices give 2,430 settings.

The ridge grid uses the same five input sets, training windows and refit intervals; alpha = 1, 10, 100 or 1,000; with or without the checklist non-conflict condition; minimum absolute predicted moves of 0, 1 or 2 bp; and either a unit position or min(abs(predicted move)/5, 1). These choices give 1,440 settings. Positive forecasts mean lower yields.

For each grid, I rank the early calendar-week SR after a 0.25 bp charge per position unit. A candidate must be active in at least 25% of weeks, average at least 0.10 absolute exposure, and have positive cost-adjusted scores in both 2006-2010 and 2011-October 2016. Appendix E. The economic reasoning behind the votes

Start with the horizon. A release's level, its surprise and the price response already behind us are different observations. A weekly forecast asks what happens next. The same information can motivate continuation if adjustment is incomplete or reversal if prices overshot. The sign alone cannot identify which occurred. News research typically measures the surprise relative to prior expectations (New York Fed); this checklist does not observe that surprise.

Curve compensation. The add-duration vote after steepening is motivated by the possibility that the extra yield farther out the curve attracts demand. Carry and roll are the intended intuition. But a change in 2s10s does not calculate either return, and a constant-maturity ten-year yield does not follow a bond as it ages. Steepening can occur with rising yields, falling yields, or a curve that remains inverted. It does not by itself imply policy easing. Curve positions and outright duration are distinct choices (CFA Institute).

Inflation and employment. Lower core inflation and unemployment above its recent norm are treated as conditions that may favor a lower future policy path. The fixed 2% core-PCE cutoff is a research convention. The formal Fed target concerns headline PCE; the numerical goal was announced in 2012, and maximum employment has no corresponding fixed numerical goal (Federal Reserve). The five-year unemployment average does not estimate the natural rate. Neither rule measures the next policy decision's surprise.

Yield compared with past inflation. I retain nominal yield minus trailing core inflation as a simple comparison with experienced inflation. The ten-year yield and past twelve-month inflation have different horizons. Its sign does not establish expected purchasing-power returns or whether a bond is cheap. The retained directional vote must stand on its empirical results.

Market prices and risk. Equity gains may signal easier conditions or stronger growth, motivating higher-yield votes. They do not demonstrate money leaving bonds. The dollar vote instead hypothesizes tighter conditions and less inflation pressure; a repricing toward higher US policy rates can lift both the dollar and yields. VIX is option-implied equity volatility (Cboe), not Treasury buying. Its add-duration vote assumes a demand-for-safety channel that need not dominate inflation or liquidity shocks.

Energy and international rates. Rising energy prices motivate a continuation hypothesis about inflation pressure, while the 2026 watchlist explores different subsequent associations. Foreign-yield votes assume common rate forces or spillovers can persist. Archived monthly foreign yields are lagged states, not contemporaneous market quotes, observed Treasury purchases, or a demonstrated lead.

Calendar and additional readings. December is a seasonal hypothesis; the calendar does not measure liquidity or imply a sell-off. Its later record contains 45 weeks from 10 Decembers. Fed and first-Friday flags identify events, not the signs of surprises. The selected below-300-day-average rule bets on a yield rebound, so its name should not be mistaken for trend following. Breakeven inflation includes risk and liquidity premia; total Fed assets include different balance-sheet operations. Volatility and price-pattern candidates similarly require explicit hypotheses. All 27 are discussed in the accompanying notes.

From a view to a portfolio. A higher probability of a yield fall need not imply a positive expected score if the less frequent rises are larger. For example, 60% odds of a 1 bp fall and 40% odds of a 3 bp rise give a long-direction expected score of -0.6 bp. The classifier's 50% cutoff is a direction rule, not an optimal duration allocation. A ten-year point forecast also does not measure a portfolio's other maturity exposures, credit spreads, carry, financing or execution costs.

Appendix F. Does next week's long-bond auction help?

An upcoming auction is information an investor can know before acting. The hypothesis is that investors may require a yield concession to absorb supply. That motivates a reduce-duration vote, but an auction can also remove uncertainty or attract demand. This weekly test covers both the period before the auction and the adjustment afterward. It cannot identify the mechanism or an auction-day trading return.

I specified four flags before calculating their results: a 20-year nominal auction, a 30-year nominal auction, a 30-year TIPS auction, or any of the three. Reopenings count by original maturity. A flag is one only when the auction falls after the signal Friday and no later than the following Friday, and its official announcement is dated strictly before the signal Friday. Same-Friday announcements are excluded. I use no auction result, bid-to-cover ratio, tail, eventual demand or backfilled tentative schedule. Each flag is tried as one add or reduce vote alongside the thirteen rules and as a 0/1 regression input. All eight combinations keep annual refits and unit positions.

The early ranking uses the highest calendar-week gross t-statistic, with at least 20 early event weeks. Settings and results are saved before the later comparisons. This is a disclosed November 2026 extension after seeing the earlier research, not an untouched holdout. The 20-year nominal bond returned in May 2020 and has no early events, so neither of its signs qualifies for early selection. The any-auction flag changes composition when that maturity enters; its early history tests 30-year nominal bonds and TIPS only.
