How the GMAT Focus Edition Sections Work

The GMAT Focus Edition has three sections: Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning and Data Insights. This page summarizes the structure published by the Graduate Management Admission Council (GMAC) and explains how the four-question mini quiz in GMAT Mini relates to it. The exam can change, so check the GMAC exam overview for the current format before you plan your preparation.

The Structure at a Glance

SectionQuestionsMinutesSkill assessed (per GMAC)
Quantitative Reasoning2145Problem solving
Verbal Reasoning2345Critical reasoning and reading comprehension
Data Insights2045Data literacy

In total that is 64 questions in 2 hours and 15 minutes, with no writing assessment. GMAC states that candidates can take the sections in whatever order they prefer, and can bookmark questions for review and change up to three answers per section. Quantitative Reasoning

This section tests problem solving with numbers, algebra and arithmetic reasoning. In GMAT Mini the Quantitative Reasoning questions are Problem Solving questions: you read a question, pick one answer choice, then read the steps that lead to the correct one. A worked example is on the practice questions page.

Verbal Reasoning

Verbal Reasoning combines two question types. Critical Reasoning gives you a short argument and asks you to reason about it, for example to find what strengthens it, weakens it or what it assumes. Reading Comprehension gives you a passage and asks questions about what it says and implies. GMAT Mini includes both types, and the explanation after each answer says why the correct choice fits and why the others do not.

Data Insights

Data Insights is the section that tests data literacy. GMAT Mini covers five question types in it:

  • Data Sufficiency: decide whether the information given is enough to answer a question.
  • Table Analysis: use a table of data to judge statements.
  • Graphics Interpretation: read a chart or graph and complete statements about it.
  • Two-Part Analysis: find two linked answers to one problem.
  • Multi-Source Reasoning: combine information from several sources, such as text and tables, to answer questions.

How a GMAT Mini Mini Quiz Maps onto the Sections

One mini quiz has four questions: one from Quantitative Reasoning, one from Verbal Reasoning, one from Data Insights and a fourth from a section that rotates, so over several mini quizzes all three sections get equal attention. If you want to work on one section only, you can choose a single subject and get four questions from it. A mini quiz is much shorter than a real section, which has 20 to 23 questions, so treat it as a daily practice habit and not as a rehearsal of exam timing. Mini quizzes do not predict a GMAT score.

Questions and Answers

How many sections are on the GMAT Focus Edition?

Three: Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning and Data Insights. GMAC lists the exam as 2 hours 15 minutes with 64 questions and no writing assessment.

Can I take the sections in any order?

GMAC says candidates can take the exam in whatever section order they prefer.

Does a GMAT Mini mini quiz copy one real section?

No. A mini quiz is four questions, one from each section plus one from a rotating section. It is a short practice habit, not a timed simulation of a section.

Where should I check the current exam format?

On the Graduate Management Admission Council website, because the test maker can change the format.
