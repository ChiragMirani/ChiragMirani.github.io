Worked Example: Problem Solving

These examples were written for this page to show the style of question and explanation. They are not GMAC questions.

Question. A shop sells pens for $3 each and notebooks for $5 each. Maria buys 8 items in total and spends $32. How many notebooks does she buy?

Explanation. Let n be the number of notebooks, so she buys 8 minus n pens. The total cost is 3 times (8 minus n) plus 5 times n, which equals 24 plus 2n. Set 24 plus 2n equal to 32, so 2n equals 8 and n equals 4. Check: 4 pens cost $12, 4 notebooks cost $20, and $12 plus $20 is $32. She buys 4 notebooks.

Worked Example: Data Sufficiency

A Data Sufficiency question does not ask you to find a value. It asks whether the statements give enough information to answer the question. The standard answer choices are: (A) statement 1 alone is sufficient, (B) statement 2 alone is sufficient, (C) both together are sufficient but neither alone is, (D) each alone is sufficient, (E) even together they are not sufficient.

Question. Is x greater than 5? Statement 1: x squared is greater than 25. Statement 2: x is greater than 0.

Explanation. Statement 1 alone: x could be 6 (answer yes) or negative 6 (answer no), so it is not sufficient. Statement 2 alone: x could be 1 (no) or 6 (yes), so it is not sufficient. Together: x is positive and its square is greater than 25, so x is greater than 5. The answer is always yes, so the statements together are sufficient. The correct choice is (C).

Verbal and Data Insights Questions

Critical Reasoning and Reading Comprehension questions reward a different habit: identify what the question is asking before you read the answer choices, and decide why each wrong choice fails, not only why the right one works. For the Data Insights types that use tables, charts or several sources, read what is given first, then read the question. GMAT Mini's explanations follow this pattern, so reading them after every answer is part of the practice.

Reviewing a Miss

  1. Read the explanation fully, even when you guessed correctly.
  2. Write down in one line why you missed it: a concept gap, a careless step or misreading the question.
  3. Mark the question. GMAT Mini keeps the questions you missed or marked in the Review tab, and you can take a mini quiz made from the questions you missed.
  4. Try again a few days later, without looking at the explanation first.
